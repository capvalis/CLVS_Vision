# Spend Edge Compute on Pixels, Not Parameters


**Bottom line.** For the smallest military targets (sub-16-pixel vehicles, drones, people, weapons, aircraft and vessels) on sub-25 W hardware, the winning open-source stack is not the newest architecture but a small, NPU-friendly CNN detector (YOLOv8n/11n/26n or the aerial-specialised LEAF-YOLO and FBRT-YOLO) on fixed-function NPUs, and the Apache-2.0 D-FINE/DEIM-N/S transformers on Jetson Orin Nano, with the compute budget deliberately redirected from "more parameters" to "more effective pixels" and temporal context. The evidence is lopsided: on AI-TOD-v2, where **86% of objects are under 16 px**, the 59M-parameter YOLOv12-X reaches only **16.1 AP** while the 11.6M-parameter D-FINE-S reaches **30.1 AP** ([Dome-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2)), and a plain YOLO11s **doubles its VisDrone AP_small (0.111 to 0.223)** merely by moving from 640 to 1280 px input ([tanirim benchmark](https://github.com/tanirim/aerial-object-detection)). Ranked by accuracy per watt, the techniques to combine are: free training-time gains first (slice-aided fine-tuning, distillation from a stride-4 P2 teacher worth **+3 to +5 mAP50 at zero runtime cost**, Objects365 pretraining worth **+3.6 AP_small**), then a Soft/cluster-NMS swap (**+2 to +6 mAP@0.5** in dense scenes), then a CPU frame-differencing motion branch with a tiny-box-aware tracker (**+7 to +8 points** in the Anti-UAV infrared ablations, more than any second CNN), then a global-to-local ROI cascade (**recall 0.56 to 0.83** on sub-150-px aircraft at 21.6 FPS on Xavier NX), then batched 4-6-tile SAHI (**+5 to +7 AP50** inference-only, 4-7x cost), then RGB-thermal late fusion (+1.9 mAP, concentrated at night), and only last a heterogeneous WBF ensemble (+1 to +3 mAP for 2x compute). The recommended pipeline is a single quantisation-friendly detector trained on tiles with a P2 head or 960-1280 input, run sparsely with a tracker filling gaps, with tiling or a local-ROI second pass triggered only when the scene demands it. Test beds should be AI-TOD-v2 and xView small-vehicle subsets for satellite, IRSTD-1K and Anti-UAV410/CST Anti-UAV for infrared, LRDDv3 and Drone-vs-Bird for RGB drones, TinyPerson and SeaDronesSee for personnel, and VisDrone/UAVDT as the general proxy, but nearly all of these are non-commercial, every Ultralytics model is AGPL-3.0, and no commercially clean, small-object-validated "military" checkpoint exists, so plan on training from permissive base weights (D-FINE, DEIM v1, RT-DETR, YOLOX) on your own or CC-licensed data.

## Sub-16-pixel targets collapse every detector, and resolution is the first lever

The scale regime matters more than the object class. On the benchmarks that actually isolate the problem, state of the art is still poor: the best published very-tiny AP on AI-TOD-v2 (objects 2-8 px) is **20.8 AP_vt** from Dome-DETR-L with the ReDHF feature-revival head, and the best extremely-small AP on SODA-D is **21.4 AP_eS**, both from a 39M-parameter, 415-GFLOP model with no code released ([ReDHF](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07994)). The SODA benchmark authors found that every detector collapses in the smallest bin (Cascade R-CNN 14.1 AP_eS, RetinaNet 11.9) ([SODA](https://shaunyuan22.github.io/SODA/)). The mechanism is well understood: a one-pixel localisation error that drops IoU from 0.81 to 0.66 on a normal object drops it from 0.39 to 0.14 on a tiny one ([SME-YOLO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11402)), and every stride-2 downsampling stage discards the few pixels of signal that remain ([arXiv 2503.20516](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20516)). A military tank at 0.3 m GSD is roughly 25x12 px and at 0.5 m roughly 14x7 px, which puts satellite vehicle detection squarely in the AI-TOD regime; xView's small cars have a **median side of about 14 px** ([Picterra](https://medium.com/picterra/the-xview-dataset-and-baseline-results-5ab4a1d0f47f)), TinyPerson's median person is **13-15 px** ([Scale Match](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.10664)), Drone-vs-Bird's drones are mostly **under 32x32 px** ([SynDroneVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05633v1)), and IRSTD-1K thermal targets average **11.9 px** ([Na-IRSTD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05804)).

Across dozens of 2024-2026 ablations, the interventions rank consistently. Restoring high-resolution features wins by the widest margin: in LAF-YOLOv10's VisDrone ablation the stride-4 P2 head was the single largest gain at **+1.3 mAP50**, versus +0.6 for the fusion neck and **+0.5 for the Wise-IoU loss swap** ([LAF-YOLOv10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13378)). A P2 head is parameter-cheap but compute-heavy, adding **only 3.5% parameters but 55.7% GFLOPs** to a YOLOv8-style model ([SSCW-YOLO](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399548381_SSCW-YOLO_A_Lightweight_and_High-Precision_Model_for_Small_Object_Detection_in_UAV_Scenarios)), and on an Orin Nano the 160x160 feature map dominates memory bandwidth, dropping LAF-YOLOv10 from 32 to **24.3 FPS** ([LAF-YOLOv10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13378)). Raising input resolution has the same effect at the same cost class: SAHI-style tiling lifted YOLO11s from 0.355/0.115 to **0.425 mAP50 / 0.176 AP_small** on VisDrone but took 510-560 ms per image instead of 73 ms ([tanirim](https://github.com/tanirim/aerial-object-detection)). Removing or shrinking the deep P5 stage is nearly free and often helps: FBRT-YOLO's redesign cut YOLOv8-S from 11.2M parameters and 28.6 GFLOPs to **2.9M and 22.9 GFLOPs while raising VisDrone AP from 23.6 to 25.9** ([FBRT-YOLO](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20670v1)).

Loss-function and label-assignment changes, by contrast, are second-order. Normalized Wasserstein Distance remains the standard tiny-object metric, but two 2025 papers found it hurts when used alone: a YOLOv10 UAV study got its best result from a **blended IoU + NWD loss (mAP@0.5 0.497)** while NWD-only underperformed plain IoU ([Sci. Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-14880-6)), and HDMamba-YOLO found no benefit over CIoU at all ([HDMamba-YOLO](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23061v1)). Receptive-field assignment (RFLA, 24.8 AP vs 20.8 for NWD on AI-TOD with DetectoRS) is the strongest assignment fix for two-stage detectors but is rarely ported to YOLO heads ([Dome-DETR Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2)). Training-only modules are the exception worth prioritising because they cost nothing at inference: plugging the contrastive DN-FPN into YOLOv8s raised AI-TOD AP from **11.6 to 18.7 at an unchanged 169.5 FPS** ([DNTR](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05755)). Explicit super-resolution front-ends are a poor use of edge FLOPs: 2x SRGAN gave about +5 points on small objects but 4x SR reduced accuracy ([Sensors and Materials 2025](https://sensors.myu-group.co.jp/sm_pdf/SM4080.pdf)), and the same pixel budget spent on native resolution or tiles delivers the gain without a second network.

The architectural frontier has shifted to density-guided sparse compute, which is the edge-compatible form of "no downsampling". Dome-DETR spends attention only where a 0.8M-parameter density extractor finds clustered tiny objects, so its compute swings from 123 GFLOPs in sparse scenes to 194 in dense ones, and the 13.2M-parameter S variant reaches **33.3 AP on AI-TOD-v2, beating the 58.7M-parameter DQ-DETR by 3.1 AP at roughly a tenth of the FLOPs** ([Dome-DETR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2)). ESOD applies the same idea to YOLO-style detectors with a sparse-convolution head for 1080p-plus inputs ([ESOD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16424)), and DenseScout frames tiny-object edge detection explicitly as a selection problem, profiled on Jetson Orin NX and RK3588 ([DenseScout](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25300)). These are the right direction, but none of them has published Jetson Orin Nano or NPU latency, so for a shipping system today they inform the pipeline design (detect sparsely, zoom selectively) rather than supply a deployable model.

## Five model families cover the three hardware tiers

The hardware splits into three tiers with different winners, because toolchain support, not raw TOPS, decides what runs. On fixed-function NPUs (Hailo-8/8L on the Raspberry Pi AI HAT+, Rockchip RK3588/RK3576, Coral) only pure-convolutional YOLOs are first-class citizens; on Jetson Orin Nano the TensorRT FP16 path makes small DETRs viable; on bare CPUs (Pi 5, phones) only nano CNNs are practical. ### Jetson Orin Nano: D-FINE/DEIM-S for accuracy, YOLO-nano for throughput

The $249 Orin Nano Super (67 sparse TOPS, 102 GB/s) is the most flexible and best-documented target ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-developer-kit-gets-a-super-boost/)). It runs YOLOv8n at **225 FPS FP16 at 14.1 W** engine-only ([hokwangchoi](https://hokwangchoi.com/blog/vision-benchmarks/)), which leaves enormous headroom for higher resolution or tiling, and it is the only low-cost platform where deformable-attention DETRs deploy cleanly through TensorRT. That matters for the tiniest targets because the DETR advantage is largest precisely there: at S scale on COCO, DEIM-S (30.4 AP_S) and D-FINE-S (29.1) beat YOLO11-S (28.7), YOLOv10-S (26.8) and YOLOv8-S (25.7) at equal or lower FLOPs ([D-FINE paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13842); [DEIMv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20787)), and on VisDrone at 640 the DEIM-D-FINE-S adaptations (HF-D-FINE +3.2 AP over D-FINE-S, HMF-DEIM +2.1 mAP50, SCA-DEIM 23.4 AP / 13.9 APs) form a real-time DETR tier at 21-24 AP ([HF-D-FINE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885625004226); [HMF-DEIM](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/7/2187); [SCA-DEIM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12788032)). Two caveats temper the DETR recommendation. First, INT8 is hazardous: NVIDIA states RT-DETR encoder/decoder/head layers are "especially sensitive" to INT8 ([NVIDIA TAO forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/rt-detr-tao-6-x-int8-accuracy-drops-than-fp16-fp32/375782)), an RF-DETR INT8 build forced through TensorRT ran 17% slower than FP16 and lost over 75% of its mAP ([dev.to](https://dev.to/khadimhussain/tensorrt-int8-vs-fp16-on-rf-detr-measured-on-an-rtx-5090-3med)), and LW-DETR on VisDrone showed AP_S suffering the largest PTQ drop, with QAT recovering 1.9-3.1 mAP ([arXiv 2607.24981](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24981)). Since INT8 buys only 1.1-1.2x over FP16 for nano models on Jetson anyway ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)), ship FP16 here. Second, COCO-tuned hyperparameters do not always transfer: D-FINE underperformed RT-DETR on the RF100-VL domain benchmark ([RF-DETR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09554)), so re-tune on the target domain. RF-DETR itself is a poor fit for tiny targets despite strong COCO numbers: its "Nano" carries a 30.5M-parameter DINOv2 backbone and gets its speed from 384-px input ([RF-DETR](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr)), the opposite of what sub-16-px objects need.

### Fixed-function NPUs: pure-conv YOLO only, with INT8 calibration as the real engineering

On Hailo, Rockchip and Coral the model choice is constrained by operator support before accuracy enters the picture. Deformable attention relies on grid_sample, which is unsupported on RKNN ([rknn-toolkit2 issue #276](https://github.com/airockchip/rknn-toolkit2/issues/276)) and is "the sticking point" for Hailo HEF export ([rf-detr issue #1024](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr/issues/1024)); the public Hailo-8L zoo contains no RT-DETR, D-FINE or DEIM, and the only transformer in it (DETR-ResNet18) takes the zoo's largest quantisation hit (-2.4 mAP) ([Hailo-8L zoo](https://github.com/hailo-ai/hailo_model_zoo/blob/master/docs/public_models/HAILO8L/HAILO8L_object_detection.rst)). Attention-flavoured YOLOs suffer the same way: yolov12n runs at **38 FPS on Hailo-8L versus 157 FPS for yolov11n**, a 4x penalty ([Hailo-8L zoo](https://github.com/hailo-ai/hailo_model_zoo/blob/master/docs/public_models/HAILO8L/HAILO8L_object_detection.rst)). The practical shortlist is therefore YOLOv8n/s, YOLO11n/s and YOLO26n/s, or an aerial-specialised CNN such as LEAF-YOLO or FBRT-YOLO re-exported through the same toolchains. INT8 losses on these CNNs are modest when calibrated by the vendor flow (Hailo: yolov8n -0.6, yolov11n -1.5, yolo26s -2.2 mAP; RKNN hybrid: -1.4), but naive TensorRT entropy PTQ cost YOLO26n 3 points ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/); [Ultralytics RKNN](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/rockchip-rknn/)), and a YOLO11n UAV study found FP16 lossless but INT8 PTQ causing "substantial" degradation concentrated on small targets ([scientia.report](https://previous.scientia.report/index.php/archive/article/view/4440/4491)). The mechanistic reason, low-magnitude high-variance activations in shallow feature maps being crushed by per-tensor scales ([LIACS thesis](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2025-2026-JohannsenL.pdf)), argues for keeping the stem and P2/P3 branches at 16 bits, which Hailo's per-layer `precision_mode=a16_w16` and RKNN's `auto_hybrid` both allow ([RidgeRun Hailo model scripts](https://developer.ridgerun.com/wiki/index.php/Hailo/Hailo-8/AI_Software_and_Tools/Hailo_Model_Scripts)). Among the NPUs, the Hailo-8 (26 TOPS, $110 HAT) is the strongest Pi companion: yolov8n hits a chip-bound **1036 FPS** and yolov8s 491 FPS because they fit a single context, versus 201/110 on the 13-TOPS Hailo-8L ([Hailo-8 zoo](https://github.com/hailo-ai/hailo_model_zoo/blob/master/docs/public_models/HAILO8/HAILO8_object_detection.rst)), and it is the only Pi accelerator with a published 1280-px run (yolov5m6 at 32.7 FPS). RK3588 is cheapest per unit but a single NPU core does yolov8n at 76 FPS INT8, so real throughput requires one RKNN context per core and a zero-copy MPP-to-RGA-to-NPU pipeline ([rknn_model_zoo](https://github.com/airockchip/rknn_model_zoo); [rk3588s-hetero-detect-engine](https://github.com/Jack-GPU/rk3588s-hetero-detect-engine)). Coral should be excluded from new designs: its compiler has been frozen at v16 since 2022, YOLO11 export fails, most 640-px ops fall back to CPU, and the maintainers themselves say "significant accuracy loss is sadly expected" ([Ultralytics Coral guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/coral-edge-tpu-on-raspberry-pi/); [Ultralytics Edge TPU](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/edge-tpu/)). The Axera AX650N is the one low-cost NPU advertising native transformer support (yolov8s at 3.64 ms), making it the candidate if a DETR must run without a Jetson ([AXERA-TECH](https://huggingface.co/AXERA-TECH/YOLOv8); [Axera](https://www.axera-tech.com/en/news/2813.html)).

### CPU-only Pi 5 and phones: nano CNNs at 7-15 FPS, with distillation as the lever

A bare Raspberry Pi 5 runs YOLO26n at **67 ms (about 15 FPS) through NCNN**, roughly twice as fast as ONNX Runtime, and anything larger than the s size is impractical ([Ultralytics RPi guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/raspberry-pi/)). The runtime choice matters more than the model generation: YOLO26's advertised 43% CPU speedup over YOLO11n was measured on a Xeon and shrinks to about 15% on the Pi ([Ultralytics YOLO26](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo26/)). Because there is no spare compute, the way to improve tiny-object accuracy here is to change the weights, not the graph: the CSCWD paper distilled a YOLO11m-P2 teacher into YOLO11n and measured on a Pi 5 (NCNN FP16) **50.3 vs 47.8 mAP@0.5 at an identical 82 ms / 12.15 FPS at 640**, and **52.9 vs 48.0 at 960 px (5.45 FPS)** on drone-vs-bird data ([CSCWD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30395)). On phones, the open-vocabulary YOLOE-11-S runs at 73 FPS on an iPhone 12 and collapses to the cost of plain YOLO11-S once re-parameterised to a fixed class list ([YOLOE](https://github.com/THU-MIG/yoloe)), but zero-shot open-vocabulary YOLOs are useless on aerial imagery (F1 under 4% on LAE-80C) ([arXiv 2601.22164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22164)), so they serve as labeling or warm-start tools, not as deployed detectors. The legacy Jetson Nano and original Coral belong in the same end-of-life bucket; the 2024-2026 literature has no benchmark of any modern family on the Maxwell Nano.

## Training-time tricks and an NMS swap deliver the cheapest accuracy

Before spending a single extra millisecond of inference, four training-side changes together are worth more than any runtime ensemble. The first is pretraining data: with Objects365 pretraining, D-FINE-S rises from **29.1 to 32.7 AP_small** and RT-DETR-R18 from 28.4 to 33.2, a bigger jump than any architectural difference between the S-scale families ([D-FINE paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13842)). For small military targets, where labelled data is scarce, this implies that the base checkpoint and the breadth of aerial pretraining (VisDrone, AI-TOD, DOTA, xView, plus the community military sets) will dominate the choice between YOLO and DETR heads. The one caution is licensing: D-FINE's own README flags the Objects365-pretrained checkpoints as possibly subject to Objects365 dataset terms ([D-FINE](https://github.com/Peterande/D-FINE)).

The second is slice-aided fine-tuning. SAHI's original ablation shows that tiled inference alone gives **+5.1 to +6.8 AP50** on VisDrone, but training the same detectors on slices roughly doubles that to **+12.7 to +14.5 AP50** at no additional inference cost beyond the tiling itself ([SAHI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.06934)). The slice-train-only variant is even more attractive for edge: YOLOv8-SMOT trains on 1280-px tiles from 4K bird footage and then infers on the full frame with no stitching, letting a YOLOv8-S reach 53.8 SO-HOTA at 17.6 FPS on 2160x3840 input, close to a YOLOv8-L at 5.7 FPS ([YOLOv8-SMOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12087)). Copy-paste augmentation of targets onto empty backgrounds, which the Drone-vs-Bird 2023 and 2025 winners both used ([Laroca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19347); [ICASSP 2023 review](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10475518)), belongs in the same bucket.

The third is distillation from a high-resolution teacher, which is the only technique in this survey that raises small-object accuracy at literally zero inference cost. CSCWD's result deserves emphasis because of its negative control: distilling from a plain YOLO11m teacher *hurt* the YOLO11n student (44.1 vs 47.3 baseline mAP@0.5), a same-scale P2 teacher gave 48.1, and cross-scale alignment of the teacher's P2 features to the student's P3 gave **50.2, beating a YOLO11s that costs 3.4x the FLOPs**, with +1.8 mAP50 zero-shot transfer to DUT-Anti-UAV ([CSCWD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30395)). Progressive chains add a little more: distilling YOLOv7-Tiny through an X-to-L-to-Tiny chain lifted VisDrone AP50 from 21.95 to 24.68 ([arXiv 2408.11407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11407)). The lesson is that the teacher must see tiny objects at the stride where they live, so a 1280-px or P2-head teacher trained on the same tiles is the right teacher, not simply a bigger model. Foundation models fit here as teachers (DINOv2-to-YOLOv8 head distillation, DisDop's RemoteCLIP/DINOv3 priors) or as annotation tools, but no paper yet reports AI-TOD or VisDrone AP_vt gains from them ([Fourret et al.](https://www.lirmm.fr/~chaumont/publications/ICIP-2025-FOURRET-CHAUMONT-FIORIO-SUBSOL-BRAU-DinoDistillationIntoYoloV8_ForFSD.pdf); [DisDop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24639)). Grounding DINO auto-labeling works as a pipeline but the teacher "struggles with small objects", so run it on tiles and review the output ([autodistill](https://github.com/autodistill/autodistill)).

The fourth is structural and free at inference: training-only modules such as DN-FPN (+7.1 AP on AI-TOD for YOLOv8s at unchanged FPS) ([DNTR](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05755)) and ReDHF's ellipse-assigned shallow supervision head, which is dropped after training ([ReDHF](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07994)). Blending NWD into the IoU loss and using an Inner/Focaler modulation is worth doing but expect under 1 AP ([LAF-YOLOv10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13378)).

The one near-free *runtime* change is replacing hard NMS. In dense aerial scenes, where tiny objects cluster, Soft-NMS raised HSP-YOLOv8's mAP@0.5 by **6.1 points** ([HSP-YOLOv8](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/fc3a/457027fc0c98756b362ef5248826e514b4f7.pdf)) and Soft-NMS-CIoU gave +4.4 on YOLOv5s-DSD ([Sensors 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10422290/)). The most useful head-to-head is ASAHI's table for merging a single detector's tile outputs on VisDrone-val: hard NMS 33.9 mAP at 3.06 img/s, Soft-NMS 32.3, WBF 33.4, and **cluster-DIoU-NMS 36.0 mAP at 5.26 img/s, both +2.1 mAP and 72% faster** ([ASAHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19233)). Note that in this single-model setting WBF and Soft-NMS were *worse* than plain NMS; WBF is for merging different models or views, and applying it to one model's output degrades results (0.453 vs 0.490 in the WBF authors' own test) ([ZFTurbo/Weighted-Boxes-Fusion](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Weighted-Boxes-Fusion)). On NPUs, NMS and DFL decoding run on the host CPU anyway (RKNN's tail.onnx, Hailo's `engine=cpu`), so swapping the algorithm costs nothing in the accelerator graph ([Ultralytics RKNN](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/rockchip-rknn/)); on Jetson, YOLO26's NMS-free head removes the step entirely at a 0.6-0.8 mAP cost ([Ultralytics YOLO26](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo26/)).

## Temporal cues, cascades and tiling buy recall at 1.5-7x cost; ensembles come last

Once the free gains are banked, every further technique multiplies inference cost, and the published ablations rank them clearly. The table summarises the expected gain and the cost multiplier of each, drawing only on measurements from the sources cited in the text that follows.

| Technique | Measured gain (benchmark) | Cost multiplier vs single pass | Edge evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slice-aided fine-tuning + P2-teacher distillation | +7 to +9 AP50 extra over tile inference alone (VisDrone); +3 to +5 mAP50 (drone-vs-bird) | 1.0x | Pi 5: 12 FPS at identical latency ([CSCWD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30395)) |
| Soft/cluster-DIoU NMS instead of hard NMS | +2.1 to +6.1 mAP@0.5 (VisDrone, UAV) | 1.0x (CPU post-processing) | [ASAHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19233) |
| CPU frame-differencing motion branch + tiny-box tracker | +7.7 (Z-Y), +8.4 global re-detection (USTC), +9 to +12 temporal scales (FudanDML) on Anti-UAV IR; tracker tweaks 44.2 to 55.2 SO-HOTA | ~1.2-1.5x (CPU ms) | GL-YOMO 21.6 FPS Xavier NX ([GL-YOMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02582)) |
| Global-to-local ROI cascade | Recall 0.56 to 0.83, AP 0.553 to 0.828 on 1-146 px fixed-wing targets | ~2x once locked | 21.6 FPS Xavier NX ([GL-YOMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02582)) |
| Sparse detection + tracker (frame skipping) | Retains 97.2% of per-frame precision | Raises system FPS <7 to 35 on native 1080p | Orin Nano ([SDG-Track](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04883v1)) |
| 4-6-tile ASAHI/SAHI + full frame, batched | +5 to +7 AP50 inference-only; +18.7 mAP@0.5 (Drone-vs-Bird 2025) | 4-7x | Orin Nano YOLOv8s INT8 43-46 ms/img, ~15 FPS with ByteTrack ([tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi](https://github.com/HouYanSong/tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi)); Pi 5 + Hailo-8L 12 tiles 8 FPS |
| RGB-thermal late fusion (average/WBF of two detectors) | 42.3 RGB to 44.2 mAP (early 43.7, mid 45.8) on DroneVehicle; night 35.2 to 44.2 (mid) | 2x | No nano RGB-T edge FPS published |
| Heterogeneous 2-model WBF ensemble | +0.7 to +3 mAP (2 models); +8 (5 models) | 2x per model | None on Orin Nano / Pi 5 / RK3588 |
| Flip / multi-scale TTA | +1 to +2 mAP (COCO) | 2-6x | Competition tool only |

Temporal processing is the best value after the free wins, and for infrared counter-UAS it is essential. Every top entry in the 3rd Anti-UAV challenge was a detector plus a motion branch: Z-Y's YOLOv5s went from **53.6 to 61.3** by feeding a retinal-motion map into the features, USTC's global re-detection was the single largest tracker gain at **+8.4**, and FudanDML's multi-frame differences as input channels lifted its score from 0.679 to 0.769-0.803, five to ten times the gain of spatial TTA ([3rd Anti-UAV report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07290)). The 4th Anti-UAV Track 1 winner in 2025 is literally titled "A Simple Detector with Frame Dynamics is a Strong Tracker" ([CVPRW 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/Anti-UAV/html/Peng_A_Simple_Detector_with_Frame_Dynamics_is_a_Strong_Tracker_CVPRW_2025_paper.html)). For a fixed or slowly panning camera, frame differencing or background modelling is a CPU-only "second detector" that costs milliseconds; GL-YOMO's three-frame LK-aligned differencing fallback added +5.7 recall points on top of its cascade while the whole system ran at 21.6 FPS on a Xavier NX ([GL-YOMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02582)). Tracker choice matters less than the association metric for tiny boxes: YOLOv8-SMOT's ablation took an OC-SORT base from **44.2 to 55.2 SO-HOTA** by adding EMA velocity, 2x box expansion and a centre-distance cost, because raw inter-frame IoU for its targets had a 10th percentile of 0.048 ([YOLOv8-SMOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12087)). Use ByteTrack or OC-SORT without an appearance model; DeepSORT's ReID network is a known 10-FPS bottleneck on Orin Nano ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/slow-fps-on-orin-nano-8-gb-yolov8/280071)). Tracking also unlocks frame skipping: SDG-Track showed YOLOv11 on native 1080p runs below 7 FPS on Orin Nano even in TensorRT FP16, but an observer-follower scheme that detects sparsely and tracks in between reaches **35.1 FPS while retaining 97.2% of per-frame precision** ([SDG-Track](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04883v1)). Be deliberate about direction: temporal interpolation trades a few false positives for recall, whereas motion-consistency filters trade recall for precision (one RT-DETR + ByteTrack fusion dropped recall from 90% to 76% to suppress false alarms) ([Motion-consistent temporal fusion](https://ait.knu.ua/en/article/view/9132)).

Cascades convert the tiling multiplier into one or two passes once a target is found. The classic cluster-then-zoom methods (ClusDet, DMNet, CRENet, Focus&Detect) remain the VisDrone accuracy leaders at up to 42.0 mAP but run at 0.7-2 images per second on desktop GPUs with ResNeXt-101 or HRNet backbones ([ASAHI SOTA table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19233)), so they are out of reach. The edge-feasible version is GL-YOMO's pattern: a nano global detector on the resized full frame, a switch to a 300x300 local ROI with a second small detector after 30 consecutive hits, and reversion after 60 misses, which raised F1 from 0.709 to 0.902 on targets averaging 0.01% of the frame ([GL-YOMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02582)). The paradigm's known weakness is that whatever the coarse pass misses is never recovered ([AdaZoom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10409)), which is why it should be paired with a recall-oriented low threshold or the motion proposer rather than used alone.

Tiling is the single largest *inference-only* gain, but it is expensive and must be engineered, not scripted. Beyond SAHI's +5 to +7 AP50 on VisDrone, the Drone-vs-Bird 2025 winner's four 55%-overlapping tiles plus a full frame raised validation mAP@0.5 from **0.552 to 0.739**, while one of seven videos regressed from 0.741 to 0.499 because higher recall surfaced rocks and vegetation as false positives ([Laroca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19347)). On xView, full-image inference is essentially useless (TOOD 2.1 AP50) and only slicing makes detection possible at all (20.6 AP50) ([SAHI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.06934)). ASAHI's tile-count sweep locates the knee: on VisDrone-test, 6 tiles gave 28.5 mAP at 4.98 img/s, 12 tiles 29.3 at 2.98, and 15 tiles regressed to 27.2, so **6 tiles for 1080p-class imagery, 12 for 4K** is the rule ([ASAHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19233)). The stock Python `sahi` package runs slices sequentially and has been measured at 0.4 FPS on a UAV task ([ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375525004125)); the only viable edge recipe is to batch all tiles plus the downscaled full frame into one fixed-shape engine call, which a C++ TensorRT implementation did on an Orin Nano 8 GB with YOLOv8s INT8 at **43-46 ms per 1080p image, and 13-15 FPS end-to-end with ByteTrack** ([tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi](https://github.com/HouYanSong/tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi)). On Pi 5 + Hailo-8L the host CPU, not the NPU, is the limit: 12 tiles of YOLOv8n reached only 8 FPS against a 106-FPS raw NPU rate ([Hailo community](https://community.hailo.ai/t/how-to-implement-tiling-without-downsampling/17407)), so on Pi-class hosts prefer compiling a non-square HEF at camera aspect (for example 1280x720) over tiling. Content-adaptive gating pays off only in sparse scenes, which are typical of sky and open-terrain military imagery: ROI-gated SAHI gave a 6.9x speedup at 2.7% ROI coverage but was slower than full SAHI in dense scenes and lost recall whenever the proposer missed ([ROI-Gated SAHI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23923)). At equal pixel budget, one 1280-px engine pass is cheaper than four 640-px tiles plus overlap and merging; tiles win when the detector was trained at 640 scale and must see targets at their trained pixel size.

RGB-thermal fusion is worth two forward passes mainly at night. On DroneVehicle with a shared backbone, RGB-only scored 42.3 mAP (day 44.8 / night 35.2), early channel concatenation 43.7, **late fusion by averaging two separate detectors 44.2**, and the purpose-built mid-level AMSRDet 45.8 with night rising to 44.2 ([AMSRDet](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/3/817)). Late fusion therefore captures roughly 60% of the best fusion gain with zero architecture work and lets each modality be trained, quantised and swapped independently, and ERDF-Anchor's finding that 50.6% of DroneVehicle objects are more reliable in RGB and 46.8% in IR explains why a decision-level reweighting works ([ERDF-Anchor](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/ell2.70683)). Because the gains concentrate in low visibility ([ISQED](https://www.isqed.org/English/Proceedings/pdf/6C-3-170.pdf)), an edge system can run IR-only at night and RGB-only by day instead of paying double continuously; lightweight single-pass RGB-T models exist in the 14-GFLOP class (CTU-YOLO 73.9% mAP on DroneVehicle at 13.8 GFLOPs) if a fused model is preferred ([CTU-YOLO](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15020298)). For the smallest thermal targets, the infrared small-target segmentation networks (WaveTD, IRST-RTDETR, LCAE-Net at 4.86 GFLOPs and 70 FPS) are 1-5M-parameter, 256-512-px models that detect 1-10-px blobs as a single class and need a classifier or tracker behind them ([LCAE-Net](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13260)).

Multi-model WBF ensembles are the lowest return per watt and should be the last addition. The headline WBF numbers come from five diverse models on Open Images (+8 mAP over the best single model, +3.4 over NMS-merging) ([Weighted-Boxes-Fusion](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Weighted-Boxes-Fusion)); a 2025 heterogeneous-YOLO study found two models with WBF gave only 0.707 versus 0.70 with NMS, and reaching 0.756 required eight models "at a steep cost in FPS" ([Technologies 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/technologies14090530)). In the aerial tiny-object domain, the evidence is the VisDrone-DET2021 workshop, where every top-10 entry stacked multi-scale testing, multi-model ensembles and WBF to land within 0.02 mAP of each other at 39.4 ([VisDrone-DET2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021W/VisDrone/papers/Cao_VisDrone-DET2021_The_Vision_Meets_Drone_Object_Detection_Challenge_Results_ICCVW_2021_paper.pdf)), and SIA-DT's 12-weight fusion in Anti-UAV 2023 added +1.9 over its best single detector ([3rd Anti-UAV report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07290)). Gains grow with architectural diversity (a YOLO plus a DETR), so if an ensemble is affordable it should pair a CNN with a DETR, weight the fusion by per-model validation mAP ([arXiv 2509.23697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23697)), and calibrate score thresholds per model before fusing, since YOLO objectness and DETR sigmoid logits are not on the same scale. No paper measures a two-model ensemble on any of the three edge platforms, and the same 2x budget spent on a 1280-px pass or six tiles buys more AP_small on every benchmark surveyed.

## A concrete pipeline for each hardware tier

The evidence above converges on one architecture with three hardware-specific instantiations: a single quantisation-friendly detector that was *trained* to see tiny objects (tiles, P2 teacher, aerial pretraining), *run* sparsely with a tracker carrying state between detections, and *escalated* to higher effective resolution (local ROI or batched tiles) only when the scene or a motion cue demands it. Ensembling is reserved for the offline teacher, not the deployed student.

**Training recipe (common to all tiers).** Start from permissive, aerially pretrained weights: D-FINE/DEIM-S or -N on Jetson, YOLOv8n/11n/26n or LEAF-YOLO on NPUs (noting the licence section below). Pretrain on the union of VisDrone, AI-TOD-v2, DOTA-v2 small-vehicle crops, xView and, for the specific classes, DUT Anti-UAV, LRDDv3, HIT-UAV, TinyPerson and the Roboflow/Mendeley military sets, then fine-tune on your own imagery. Train on 640 or 1280 slices with 20-25% overlap ([SAHI](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.06934)), with copy-paste of real target chips onto empty backgrounds and a negative "bird/distractor" class, which improved drone AP in Drone-vs-Bird ([Laroca et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19347)). Use a stride-4 P2 head during training; for the deployed student either keep it (Jetson, if the latency budget allows the roughly 1.55x GFLOPs) or distil it away CSCWD-style by aligning the teacher's P2 features to the student's P3 ([CSCWD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30395)). Keep CIoU/SIoU as the base loss with an NWD blend. Build the teacher as the ensemble you cannot afford at runtime: a 1280-px P2 model plus a D-FINE-M, fused by WBF, generating soft targets and pseudo-labels on unlabeled footage (co-teaching with a 0.3 threshold handles label noise) ([arXiv 2511.09955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09955)). Validate every export against an FP16 export at the same input size and letterboxing, not against the PyTorch checkpoint, or quantisation loss will be confounded with preprocessing differences ([uav-yolo-edge-compression issue #9](https://github.com/Sri-Krishna-V/uav-yolo-edge-compression/issues/9)).

**Tier A, Jetson Orin Nano Super (7-25 W, the recommended default).** Run DEIM-D-FINE-S (or a D-FINE-S with the HF-D-FINE high-resolution modification) in TensorRT FP16 at 960-1280 px on the full frame; scaling from RT-DETR-R18's measured 73-81 FPS at 60 GFLOPs ([AgriJetsonBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00927v1); [arXiv 2606.14005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14005)) and the roughly 3.5-4x cost from 640 to 1280 on this bandwidth-bound part, expect 25-40 FPS engine-only at 1280, or run at 640 and tile. Decode through DeepStream with hardware NVDEC (the Orin Nano handles 4-6 1080p streams of YOLOv8 this way) ([Seeed DeepStream](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/YOLOv8-DeepStream-TRT-Jetson/)), use DeepStream-Yolo's parsers, which already support D-FINE, RT-DETR and YOLO26 ([DeepStream-Yolo](https://github.com/marcoslucianops/DeepStream-Yolo)), and keep INT8 off the table unless a QAT checkpoint from ModelOpt is available. Detect every Nth frame and run ByteTrack or OC-SORT with 2x box expansion and centre-distance cost in between. When the stream is a stationary or slow-panning sensor, add a CPU frame-difference proposer; when a track locks or the proposer fires in an unoccupied region, dispatch a batched engine call of 4-6 tiles (or a 300-600 px local crop at native resolution) through a second TensorRT profile, following the tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi batching pattern that gives roughly 15 FPS at 1080p on this device ([tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi](https://github.com/HouYanSong/tensorrtx-yolov8-sahi)). Merge tile and full-frame outputs with cluster-DIoU-NMS, not WBF. For day/night platforms, run a separately trained IR detector and fuse at decision level by validation-mAP-weighted WBF only when both sensors are active.

**Tier B, Raspberry Pi 5 + Hailo-8 AI HAT+ or RK3588 (5-13 W).** Deploy a pure-convolutional YOLOv8s/YOLO11s (or YOLOv8n on the 13-TOPS Hailo-8L) distilled from the P2 teacher and compiled INT8 with 500-1000 production-like calibration images containing tiny targets, Hailo optimisation level 2-3 with adaround, and the stem plus P2/P3 convolutions held at 16-bit ([RidgeRun Hailo model scripts](https://developer.ridgerun.com/wiki/index.php/Hailo/Hailo-8/AI_Software_and_Tools/Hailo_Model_Scripts)). Compile a non-square HEF at the camera's native aspect and the highest resolution the chip sustains (Hailo-8 has demonstrated 1280x1280 and supports `--resize 1080 1920`) ([Hailo community](https://community.hailo.ai/t/general-question-about-video-size-1080p-30-fps-and-hailo8/9308)) rather than tiling, because the Pi's decode and pre/post-processing caps end-to-end pipelines at 7-30 FPS regardless of NPU speed ([Sixfab](https://sixfab.com/blog/raspberry-pi-5-ai-hat-benchmark-deepx-vs-hailo/)). Set PCIe to Gen3 ([DataRoot Labs](https://datarootlabs.com/blog/hailo-ai-kit-raspberry-pi-5-setup-and-computer-vision-pipelines)). Put the motion branch and ByteTrack on the Pi's CPU, and use a GL-YOMO-style local ROI (one extra 640 crop per locked track) as the escalation step instead of full tiling. On RK3588, use three RKNN contexts (one per NPU core) behind a zero-copy MPP-to-RGA pipeline, which independent builds show delivering about 54 FPS aggregate yolov8n at 640 ([rk3588s-hetero-detect-engine](https://github.com/Jack-GPU/rk3588s-hetero-detect-engine)); ReLU-activated variants compile faster on this NPU. Expect VisDrone-class accuracy in the 40-48 AP50 range at 640, the realistic 2025 ceiling for sub-10 W single-pass detectors.

**Tier C, bare Pi 5 CPU or mobile CPU.** Run the distilled YOLO11n/YOLO26n through NCNN at 640 (about 12-15 FPS) or 960 (about 5.5 FPS) and rely entirely on training-side gains plus a tracker to skip frames ([CSCWD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30395); [Ultralytics RPi guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/raspberry-pi/)). There is no budget for tiling or a second model; the motion branch is the only runtime escalation that fits.

**What to expect, cumulatively.** Against a stock nano YOLO at 640 on VisDrone-class imagery (about 33-40 AP50), slice-aided training plus P2 distillation should add 3-9 AP50, aerial pretraining a further 2-4 AP_small, the NMS swap 2-6 mAP@0.5 in dense scenes, and tiling or 1280-px inference another 5-7 AP50 inference-only when the budget allows, with temporal processing adding recall of a different kind (roughly 0.56 to 0.83 on tiny aircraft) that per-frame metrics undercount. These add imperfectly rather than linearly, and every figure in this report was measured on a desktop GPU except where an edge device is named; the one combined accuracy-plus-FPS measurement on edge hardware is CSCWD's Pi 5 result, so treat the remaining stack as a well-supported plan that must be re-measured on the target device rather than as a guaranteed total.

Three realities follow. First, in-domain numbers on the community military sets are misleading: the two Hugging Face "defence" checkpoints (OKIYOLO26X-DEFENSIVE at 75.8 mAP50 under AGPL, YOLOv8m_defence at 0.598 mAP50-95 under Apache on private data) were validated on splits drawn from the same overlapping Roboflow pools and never on small objects ([OKIYOLO HF card](https://huggingface.co/theokiGuyOKI/OKIYOLO26X-DEFENSIVE); [YOLOv8m_defence](https://huggingface.co/spencercdz/YOLOv8m_defence)), and cross-dataset drone detection falls to **0.43-0.48 mAP50** when a YOLO11m trained on Drone-vs-Bird or DUT Anti-UAV is tested on Det-Fly ([LRDDv3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25942)). HERIDAL-trained person detectors scored 0.0 AP on real under-canopy footage ([ForestPersons](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02541v1)). Multi-dataset training is mandatory, and the honest internal test set is one you capture with your own sensor at operational range. Second, genuine gaps exist: there is no peer-reviewed tank or APC detection result from satellite imagery, no open SAR dataset of military ground vehicles (SARDet-100K's "tank" class is storage tanks), and essentially no real long-range UAV imagery of camouflaged or prone soldiers ([SARDet_100K](https://github.com/zcablii/SARDet_100K); [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/9z7yrcrpjk/1)). Third, only SeaDronesSee (CC0), HIT-UAV (CC0), HERIDAL (CC BY 3.0), the Halmstad multi-sensor drone set (CC BY 4.0), MTARSI (CC BY) and the Mendeley set (CC BY 4.0) are clearly commercial-compatible among the sets above; xView, DOTA, MAR20, SARDet-100K, LLVIP, Drone-vs-Bird and TinyPerson are not.

### Model licensing: AGPL on every Ultralytics path, Apache on the DETR side

The licensing picture for models is at least as consequential as the data picture. Every Ultralytics model (YOLOv5/v8/11/26) plus YOLOv10, YOLOv12, YOLOv13 and the YOLOE/YOLO-World ports are AGPL-3.0, which covers the code, the training pipeline and trained or fine-tuned weights, and whose network clause is triggered by serving a model over a network, with a paid Enterprise licence as the alternative ([Lightly](https://www.lightly.ai/blog/best-ultralytics-alternatives-in-2026); [Ultralytics YOLO26](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo26/)). Loading an Apache model such as RT-DETR through the `ultralytics` package re-imposes AGPL on that use, so Apache models must be run from their upstream repositories ([tunasakara](https://tunasakara.com/en/blog/agpl-commercial-computer-vision)). The clean Apache-2.0 set is RT-DETR/v2 (Baidu upstream), D-FINE, DEIM v1, RF-DETR Nano through Large, LW-DETR, YOLOX, NanoDet, PP-PicoDet/PP-YOLOE and DAMO-YOLO ([D-FINE](https://github.com/Peterande/D-FINE); [DEIM](https://github.com/ShihuaHuang95/DEIM); [RT-DETR](https://github.com/lyuwenyu/RT-DETR)). Traps within that set: DEIMv2 ships a custom licence that requires contacting the authors for commercial use, unlike DEIM v1 ([DEIMv2](https://github.com/Intellindust-AI-Lab/DEIMv2)); RF-DETR's Atto/Femto/Pico variants fall under a separate "PML 1.0" licence ([RF-DETR](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr)); YOLO-NAS weights are non-commercial and Deci's licensing channel closed after the NVIDIA acquisition ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/licensing-and-usage-terms-for-yolo-nas-model/326342)); LEAF-YOLO is GPL-3.0 ([LEAF-YOLO](https://github.com/highquanglity/LEAF-YOLO)); and the Hailo model-zoo retraining dockers wrap the AGPL Ultralytics repos. NVIDIA TAO's code is Apache-2.0 and most NGC weights are marked commercial-ready, but some model cards carry "Critical Applications" restrictions whose wording must be checked per model for defence use ([NVIDIA forum TAO licensing](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/understanding-tao-models-commercial-licensing/281977)). Whether dataset restrictions (DOTA's academic-only terms) propagate to weights trained on them is unsettled; Ultralytics' position that they do not is "not independently confirmed" ([ultralytics PR #27094](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/pull/27094)). The practical consequence for a commercial system is that the Jetson tier should be built on D-FINE/DEIM v1 or upstream RT-DETR, and the NPU tier on YOLOX, PP-YOLOE or an Ultralytics Enterprise licence, trained on CC-licensed and self-collected data.

## Conclusion

The 2024-2026 literature quietly overturns the instinct to chase the newest detector for tiny targets. On the benchmarks that isolate sub-16-px objects, a mid-2024 Apache-licensed DETR at 11.6M parameters beats a 2025 attention-centric YOLO at 59M by nearly 2x, and a 2.6M-parameter YOLO11n gains more from a well-chosen teacher than from tripling its FLOPs. The variables that move AP_small are effective pixel count at the detector's input, the stride at which features are supervised, the breadth of aerial pretraining, and the post-processing used to merge clustered boxes; architecture generation, loss-function novelty and runtime ensembling each contribute a point or less once those are fixed. The practical corollary for an edge budget is an inversion of the competition recipe: competitions ensemble at inference, but an edge system should ensemble only in the teacher and ship one distilled, quantisation-friendly student whose extra compute goes to resolution and temporal context.

Two developments are worth tracking because they would change the recommendation. Density-guided sparse compute (Dome-DETR, ESOD, DenseScout) is the first architectural idea that approximates full-resolution processing at a fraction of the cost and has already been profiled on Orin NX and RK3588 but not yet on Orin Nano or any NPU; the first open implementation with sub-20-ms Jetson latency would displace tiling. And transformer-native low-cost NPUs (Axera today, Hailo-10H-class parts tomorrow) plus grid_sample-free DETR variants (RT-DETRv2's discrete sampling, RF-DETR's gather rewrite) would let the D-FINE/DEIM accuracy advantage reach the Pi and Rockchip tiers that are currently confined to pure-convolutional YOLO. Until then, the largest uncertainty in this report is not which model to pick but how the stacked gains compose on the target device, because the only edge measurement that reports accuracy and FPS together for a small-object technique is a single Raspberry Pi experiment; the first week of any such project should therefore be spent building the AI-TOD-v2, VisDrone and own-sensor evaluation harness on the actual hardware, so that each of the techniques ranked here is admitted or rejected on measured AP_small per watt rather than on desktop-GPU paper numbers.
